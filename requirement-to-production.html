Project Management

From requirement to production

Operating model · 12 min read · July 2026

Most digital programmes do not fail in the code. They fail in the space between a verbal brief and a production release — where scope is assumed, priorities drift, and nobody owns the definition of done.

Across nearly nine years of engineering and delivery leadership, I have used the same operating model whether the stack is WordPress, WooCommerce, or a mixed Flutter / React programme: make the requirement explicit, wind a sprint cadence, and refuse to ship without quality gates.

1. Scope is a product

Before anyone opens Figma or a repository, document what success looks like, what is out of scope, and who decides changes. Ambiguity is the most expensive line item on a project.

  • Written acceptance criteria for each major deliverable
  • A single owner for scope changes
  • Visible risks — timezone, content readiness, third-party APIs

2. Cadence beats heroics

Agile and Scrum are not theatre. Sprint planning, backlog clarity, standups that surface blockers, and retrospectives that change behaviour keep concurrent WordPress and cross-stack work honest.

3. Quality gates before launch

QA, responsive checks, analytics hooks, and handover notes are part of the product. At COLAB and Times Mirror, delivery ownership meant the conversation did not end when the theme compiled — it ended when the client could operate the system.

What this means for buyers

If you need a Technical Delivery Lead or Project Manager who still understands the build, hire for operating discipline first. Tools change. The cost of unclear requirements does not.
